Overview

Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart is considered one of the greatest and most creative musical geniuses of all time. During his lifetime, he relentlessly solicited students and commissions. He died on December 5, 1791, possibly of rheumatic fever. He was only 35 years old. Mozart left behind huge debts, partially because he lacked the financial backing from the nobility that was necessary for a composer of his time to make a good living. Instead, he pieced together a living from selling his compositions, giving music lessons, and giving public performances.

Although he had been famous as a child prodigy, Mozart's genius as a composer was not really appreciated until after his death. His music is known today for its sheer virtuosity, beauty, and perfection. Mozart had the ability to compose both in a lighthearted and simple manner as well as in a deeply moving and profound style. His operas include memorable characters and intense drama. Unlike many other composers, Mozart could write equally well for voice, stringed instruments, wind instruments, the piano, or an entire symphony orchestra.
