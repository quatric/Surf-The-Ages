Carson, Rachel (1907-1964), was an American marine biologist and science writer. She wrote several books that reflect her lifelong interest in the life of the seas and the seashores.

In her writings, Carson stressed the interrelation of all living things and the dependence of human welfare on natural processes. The Sea Around Us (1951) describes the biology, chemistry, geography, and history of the sea. Silent Spring (1962) called public attention to the wasteful and destructive use of pesticides. Carson warned that pesticides poison the food supply of animals and kill many birds and fish. She pointed out that pesticides could also contaminate human food supplies. Her arguments helped lead to restrictions on the use of pesticides in many parts of the world.
