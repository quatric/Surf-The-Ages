Piramesse

Ramses established his capital about 71 miles (114 kilometers) northeast of the modern city of Cairo on the site of a smaller city that he renamed Piramesse, or "House of Ramses." In 1998, archaeologists excavating at Piramesse discovered the remains of Ramses's royal stables, the oldest and best-preserved stables ever found in the Middle East. The building housed about 460 horses, which were used for warfare, hunting, and recreation.
