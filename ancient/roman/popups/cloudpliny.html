Pliny the Younger is the first known observer of what scientists now call a Plinian eruption in his honor. This highly explosive type of eruption produces a huge vertical column of pumice (a volcanic glass), ash, and gases. The column actually consists of numerous jets of volcanic material roaring from a volcano. Modern examples of Plinian eruptions are those of Mount St. Helens in Washington in 1980 and Mount Pinatubo in the Philippines in 1991.
