flood

Scientists now believe that pyroclastic surges (glowing clouds of hot ash and dust) killed many of Vesuvius's victims. A 2001 study of the skeletons of 80 people killed at Herculaneum revealed that these victims died almost instantly as a surge rolled over them, burning and suffocating them. The heat was so intense--calculated at about 1700° F.--that it blackened the inside of their skulls.
